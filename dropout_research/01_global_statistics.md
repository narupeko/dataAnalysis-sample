## 1. OECD Education at a Glance (EAG): completion of bachelor's entrants (indicator B5, formerly A9/A4)

**Definition from EAG 2016 onward.** The data follow a cohort of full-time bachelor's entrants, using true-cohort or cross-cohort data. The indicator is the share who graduate from any tertiary programme:
- within the theoretical duration (N);
- within N+1 years;
- within N+3 years.

The share who "left tertiary education without graduating" is reported at the same points.

Countries do not all report; the OECD average covers only those that do. The indicator was introduced as a new indicator in EAG 2016 (A9), and became B5 from EAG 2019/2022.

## 5. Comparability caveats

1. **The OECD series changed definition.** EAG 2008–2013 used cross-sectional "survival" or completion rates, i.e. graduates divided by entrants N years earlier, for tertiary-type A. EAG 2016 onward uses cohort-based rates for full-time bachelor's entrants at N, N+1 and N+3. The two series cannot be spliced (roughly 70% before and 67–70% at N+3 after only looks similar). The countries in the OECD average also change from one edition to the next.
2. **"Completion" in B5 means any tertiary degree**, including short-cycle degrees and a switch to another bachelor's programme. "Left tertiary" excludes students who are still enrolled.
3. **IPEDS versus NSC.** IPEDS counts completion at the first institution only, for first-time full-time bachelor's-seekers. NSC counts any institution, all first-time students and any credential. For the same cohort, NSC totals include transfers that IPEDS counts as non-completers.
4. **NSC restates** its persistence back-series every year (fall 2023: 77.6% first published, 76.8% restated), and changed method around the 2018 cohort.
5. **SCNC headline definitions changed** between reports (all ages, then under 65, plus method changes), so the count is not a consistent time series.
6. **UK:** HESA non-continuation is a one-year measure for UK-domiciled students, split into young and all. It differs from OfS continuation (which counts students who qualify or are still active after 1 year plus 14 days, England only). It is also not a lifetime dropout rate.
7. **Short, fixed-length systems score higher on "within N".** Countries with 3-year bachelor's degrees and structured cohorts (UK, Ireland) look better within N than systems with flexible study length (Nordic countries, Germany, the Netherlands), where many students finish at N+1 to N+3.
8. **Dropout from a programme, from an institution and from the whole system are different measures.** DZHW, SPADIES and national ministries mostly use system-level or cohort-estimate methods, while many institutional statistics measure programme or institutional exit.

## 6. Countries not searched: background figures, all UNVERIFIED

These must be checked against primary sources before any use.

- **Germany (DZHW Studienabbruchquote, bachelor's, German students, estimated from graduation-year cohorts).**
  - Latest (reference year 2020, published 2022): about 35% at universities, about 20% at universities of applied sciences (FH), about 28% overall.
  - Earlier editions (2008/2010/2012/2014/2016/2018 reference years): roughly 25–30% overall; universities about 32–35%; FH about 19–27%.
  - Source series: dzhw.eu, "Studienabbruchquoten" (Heublein et al.).
- **France.** Taux de réussite en licence en 3 ans about 28–30%; in 3 or 4 years about 40–45%. The cohort of 2018–19 entrants rose during COVID. Source: MESR SIES "Note d'information" on réussite et assiduité en licence.
- **Australia (Department of Education, adjusted attrition rate for domestic commencing bachelor's students).** About 15% in the mid-2000s, about 12–13% around 2009, about 15% in the mid-to-late 2010s, and variable after COVID.
- **Japan (MEXT surveys of university dropout).** Annual dropout about 2.65% in fiscal 2012 (the MEXT 2014 survey) and about 2% in the early 2020s. This is an annual rate, not a cohort rate.
- **Korea.** Annual university 중도탈락률 (in-course dropout rate) about 4.5–5% (source: 대학알리미, the Higher Education in Korea information portal).
- **Colombia (SPADIES).** Cohort dropout at university level about 45% by the 10th semester; annual rate about 9–10%.
- **Chile (SIES).** First-year retention in university undergraduate programmes about 75–80%.
- **South Africa (DHET cohort studies).** Roughly 60% of students in 3-year degrees complete within 6 years.
- **Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Nordics, Canada, China, Brazil:** no data collected.
