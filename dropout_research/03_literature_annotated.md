# Annotated bibliography: university student dropout, retention and dropout prevention

I found 98 works across strands A–E. Every search was a WebSearch query. WebFetch was blocked: J-STAGE returned EGRESS_BLOCKED. The session then hit its limit of 200 WebSearch calls, so I stopped searching and built the list from the results already collected.

**Rules I followed**
- DOIs and URLs are included only when they appeared in search results. A URL built from a DOI that did appear is labelled "DOI link".
- "verify" means one of the bibliographic fields or the OA status was not confirmed.
- "OA copy (3rd-party)" means a full text hosted by someone other than the publisher or author, such as a course site or a university office. It may not be authorised and may not stay online.

**Corrections to the brief**
- Weiss et al. 2019 (CUNY ASAP long-term results) was published in *AEJ: Applied* 11(3), not *JPAM*.
- The "Oreopoulos & Petronijevic 2019 nudges at scale" item is really two papers:
- Oreopoulos & Petronijevic, "The Remarkable Unresponsiveness of College Students to Nudging…", NBER w26059.
- Bird, Castleman, Denning, Goodman, Lamberton & Rosinger, "Nudging at Scale", NBER w26158, later *JEBO* 2021.
- For "清水一彦", the searches returned **清水一** (大阪経大論集), not 清水一彦. Please verify the attribution.

## A. Classic theory and frameworks (chronological)

1. **Spady, W. G. (1970).** Dropouts from higher education: An interdisciplinary review and synthesis. *Interchange*, 1, 64–85. DOI 10.1007/BF02214313.
 - Proposes the first sociological model of dropout, based on Durkheim's theory of suicide: the student's attributes interact with the academic and social systems of the university.
 - Access: **paywalled**.
2. **Spady, W. G. (1971).** Dropouts from higher education: Toward an empirical model. *Interchange*, 2, 38–62. DOI 10.1007/BF02282469.
 - Tests the 1970 model empirically; social integration and grade performance drive the decision to leave.
 - Access: **paywalled**.
3. **Tinto, V. (1975).** Dropout from higher education: A theoretical synthesis of recent research. *Review of Educational Research*, 45(1), 89–125. DOI 10.3102/00346543045001089.
 - Sets out the student integration model: academic and social integration shape goal and institutional commitments, which in turn drive persistence. It is the most-cited framework in the field.
 - Access: **paywalled**. ERIC record EJ120200. A 1973 report version is ERIC ED078802 (full text: verify).
4. **Pascarella, E. T., & Terenzini, P. T. (1980).** Predicting freshman persistence and voluntary dropout decisions from a theoretical model. *Journal of Higher Education*, 51(1), 60–75. DOI 10.1080/00221546.1980.11780030.
 - Scales built from Tinto's model correctly classified 78.5% of 773 first-year students. Interaction with faculty was especially important.
 - Access: **paywalled**.
5. **Bean, J. P. (1980).** Dropouts and turnover: The synthesis and test of a causal model of student attrition. *Research in Higher Education*, 12, 155–187. DOI 10.1007/BF00976194.
 - First attrition model based on theories of employee turnover in organisations (N = 1,171, path analysis).
 - Access: OA copy (3rd-party, verify) at http://digitalwolfgram.widener.edu/digital/api/collection/p16069coll41/id/57442/download
6. **Astin, A. W. (1984).** Student involvement: A developmental theory for higher education. *Journal of College Student Personnel*, 25, 297–308. No DOI.
 - Involvement theory: students learn and persist in proportion to the physical and psychological energy they invest in college.
 - Access: OA copy (3rd-party) at http://chawkinson.pbworks.com/w/file/fetch/122997693/Student_Involvement_A_Development_Theory_for_Highe.pdf. ERIC EJ309521.
7. **Bean, J. P., & Metzner, B. S. (1985).** A conceptual model of nontraditional undergraduate student attrition. *Review of Educational Research*, 55(4), 485–540. DOI 10.3102/00346543055004485.
 - For older, commuter and part-time students, the external environment (finances, work, family) matters more than social integration.
 - Access: **paywalled**. ERIC EJ330749.
8. **Tierney, W. G. (1992).** An anthropological analysis of student participation in college. *Journal of Higher Education*, 63(6), 603–618. DOI: verify.
 - Critiques the assimilationist "rites of passage" reading of Tinto; argues that minority students should not have to shed their cultures to integrate.
 - Access: **paywalled**.
9. **Cabrera, A. F., Nora, A., & Castañeda, M. B. (1992).** The convergence between two theories of college persistence. *Journal of Higher Education*, 63(2). Details: verify, not retrieved by search.
 - Shows that Tinto's and Bean's models overlap and are complementary.
 - Access: **paywalled / verify**.
10. **Cabrera, A. F., Nora, A., & Castañeda, M. B. (1993).** College persistence: Structural equations modeling test of an integrated model of student retention. *Journal of Higher Education*, 64(2), 123–139. DOI: verify.
  - The integrated model finds that financial aid and attitudes, together with academic and social integration, shape persistence.
  - Access: **paywalled** (a ResearchGate copy may exist).
11. **Tinto, V. (1993).** *Leaving College: Rethinking the Causes and Cures of Student Attrition* (2nd ed.). University of Chicago Press.
  - The canonical revised interactionalist theory, with principles for effective retention programmes.
  - Access: **paywalled**. Internet Archive lending copy at https://archive.org/details/leavingcollegere0000tint. ERIC ED371658.
12. **Braxton, J. M., Hirschy, A. S., & McClendon, S. A. (2004).** *Understanding and Reducing College Student Departure.* ASHE-ERIC Higher Education Report 30(3). Jossey-Bass.
  - Revises Tinto's theory for residential campuses and proposes a separate theory for commuter institutions.
  - Access: **paywalled**. ERIC ED501184.
13. **Yorke, M., & Longden, B. (2004).** *Retention and Student Success in Higher Education.* SRHE / Open University Press.
  - Comparative UK, Australian, South African and US perspective linking retention theory to policy and institutional practice.
  - Access: **paywalled**.
14. **Kuh, G. D., Kinzie, J., Buckley, J. A., Bridges, B. K., & Hayek, J. C. (2006).** *What Matters to Student Success: A Review of the Literature.* NPEC commissioned report.
  - Framework linking pre-college experiences, student engagement and institutional conditions to student success.
  - Access: **OA** at https://www.yorku.ca/retentn/rdata/whatmatterstostudentsuccess.pdf
15. **Kuh, G. D. (2008).** *High-Impact Educational Practices: What They Are, Who Has Access to Them, and Why They Matter.* AAC&U.
  - Defines 10 high-impact practices (first-year seminars, learning communities, undergraduate research and others) that are associated with engagement and retention, especially for underserved students.
  - Access: OA copy (institutional host) at https://navigate.utah.edu/_resources/documents/hips-kuh-2008.pdf
16. **Tinto, V. (2012).** *Completing College: Rethinking Institutional Action.* University of Chicago Press.
  - Four conditions for completion: expectations, support, assessment and feedback, and involvement. The classroom is the main lever.
  - Access: **paywalled**. Publisher excerpt at https://press.uchicago.edu/books/excerpt/2012/tinto_completing_college.html. ERIC ED531591.
17. **Thomas, L. (2012).** *Building Student Engagement and Belonging in Higher Education at a Time of Change: Final Report from the What Works? Student Retention & Success Programme.* Paul Hamlyn Foundation / HEFCE / HEA.
  - Covers 22 UK institutions: belonging, developed mainly through the academic sphere, is central to retention.
  - Access: **OA** at https://www.advance-he.ac.uk/knowledge-hub/building-student-engagement-and-belonging-higher-education-time-change-final-report
18. **Tinto, V. (2017).** Through the eyes of students. *Journal of College Student Retention: Research, Theory & Practice*, 19(3), 254–269. DOI 10.1177/1521025115621917.
  - Shifts the question from what institutions do to retain students to how students decide to persist: self-efficacy, sense of belonging and perceived value of the curriculum drive motivation.
  - Access: **paywalled**. ERIC EJ1156655.
19. **Tinto, V. (2017).** Reflections on student persistence. *Student Success*, 8(2).
  - Short open-access companion piece setting out the student-motivation view of persistence.
  - Access: **OA** at https://studentsuccessjournal.org/article/view/495

## B. Meta-analyses, systematic reviews and national/European reviews

20. **Robbins, S. B., Lauver, K., Le, H., Davis, D., Langley, R., & Carlstrom, A. (2004).** Do psychosocial and study skill factors predict college outcomes? A meta-analysis. *Psychological Bulletin*, 130(2), 261–288. DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.130.2.261.
  - 109 studies. Academic goals, academic self-efficacy and academic-related skills predict retention. Self-efficacy and achievement motivation predict GPA.
  - Access: **paywalled**.
21. **Valentine, J. C., Hirschy, A. S., Bremer, C. D., Novillo, W., Castellano, M., & Banister, A. (2011).** Keeping at-risk students in school: A systematic review of college retention programs. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis*, 33(2). DOI 10.3102/0162373711398126.
  - Retention programmes have small but potentially important effects on short-term retention and grades. Comprehensive programmes evaluated with better comparison groups show larger effects.
  - Access: **paywalled**.
22. **Larsen, M. S., Kornbeck, K. P., Kristensen, R. M., Larsen, M. R., & Sommersel, H. B. (2013).** *Dropout Phenomena at Universities: What Is Dropout? Why Does Dropout Occur? What Can Be Done by the Universities to Prevent or Reduce It?* Danish Clearinghouse for Educational Research, Aarhus University (Clearinghouse research series 2013, no. 15).
  - Systematic review of European empirical studies commissioned by the Swiss council CORECHED.
  - Access: **OA** at https://edu.au.dk/fileadmin/edu/Udgivelser/Clearinghouse/Review/Evidence_on_dropout_from_universities_technical_report_May_2013.pdf
23. **Quinn, J. (2013).** *Drop-out and Completion in Higher Education in Europe among Students from Under-represented Groups.* NESET report for the European Commission.
  - Students from low socioeconomic backgrounds are by far the most likely to drop out; the report sets out policy levers.
  - Access: **OA** at https://edudoc.ch/record/110174?ln=en
24. **Heublein, U. (2014).** Student drop-out from German higher education institutions. *European Journal of Education*, 49(4), 497–513. DOI 10.1111/ejed.12097.
  - Estimates from cohort comparisons that about 28% of German bachelor's students drop out, and describes a process model based on Tinto.
  - Access: **paywalled**. ERIC EJ1044496.
25. **Vossensteyn, H., Kottmann, A., Jongbloed, B., Kaiser, F., Cremonini, L., Stensaker, B., Hovdhaugen, E., Wollscheid, S., Thomas, L., & Unger, M. (2015).** *Dropout and Completion in Higher Education in Europe: Main Report* (HEDOCE). European Commission / CHEPS / NIFU.
  - Inventory of study-success policies in 35 countries plus 8 case studies. Finds few robust impact evaluations.
  - Access: **OA** at https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/4deeefb5-0dcd-11e6-ba9a-01aa75ed71a1/language-en
26. **Aljohani, O. (2016a).** A review of the contemporary international literature on student retention in higher education. *International Journal of Education and Literacy Studies*, 4(1), 40–52.
  - International review: the quality of the institutional experience and integration are the most influential factors.
  - Access: **OA** at https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1149286.pdf
27. **Aljohani, O. (2016b).** A comprehensive review of the major studies and theoretical models of student retention in higher education. *Higher Education Studies*, 6(2).
  - Compares the Spady, Tinto, Bean, Bean–Metzner and Cabrera models.
  - Access: **OA** at https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1092026.pdf
28. **Heublein, U., Ebert, J., Hutzsch, C., Isleib, S., König, R., Richter, J., & Woisch, A. (2017).** *Zwischen Studienerwartungen und Studienwirklichkeit* (Forum Hochschule 1/2017). DZHW.
  - Large German survey of people who left their studies, covering reasons (performance problems, motivation, finances), where they ended up, and dropout rates.
  - Access: **OA** at https://www.dzhw.eu/pdf/pub_fh/fh-201701.pdf
29. **Manyanga, F., Sithole, A., & Hanson, S. M. (2017).** Comparison of student retention models in undergraduate education from the past eight decades. *Journal of Applied Learning in Higher Education*, 7, 30–42.
  - Historical comparison of retention models and the factors they rely on.
  - Access: **OA** at https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1188373.pdf
30. **Kumar, M., Singh, A. J., & Handa, D. (2017).** Literature survey on educational dropout prediction. *International Journal of Education and Management Engineering*, 7(2), 8–19.
  - Early survey of data-mining methods for dropout prediction.
  - Access: OA (publisher, verify) at https://www.mecs-press.org/ijeme/ijeme-v7-n2/v7n2-2.html
31. **Sneyers, E., & De Witte, K. (2018).** Interventions in higher education and their effect on student success: A meta-analysis. *Educational Review*, 70(2), 208–228. DOI 10.1080/00131911.2017.1300874.
  - Academic probation lowers retention (d = −0.17). Faculty mentoring raises retention (d = 0.15) and graduation (d = 0.10). Need-based grants raise enrolment, retention and graduation (d ≈ 0.05 each).
  - Access: verify. The T&F full text appeared in results and may be OA: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00131911.2017.1300874
32. **Kehm, B. M., Larsen, M. R., & Sommersel, H. B. (2019).** Student dropout from universities in Europe: A review of empirical literature. *Hungarian Educational Research Journal*, 9(2), 147–164. DOI: verify.
  - Summarises 44 European empirical studies and stresses the distinction between formal dropout and transfer.
  - Access: **OA** at https://real.mtak.hu/132512/ and https://akjournals.com/view/journals/063/9/2/article-p147.xml
33. **[Neugebauer, M., Heublein, U., & Daniel, A.: authors verify] (2019).** Studienabbruch in Deutschland: Ausmaß, Ursachen, Folgen, Präventionsmöglichkeiten. *Zeitschrift für Erziehungswissenschaft*. DOI 10.1007/s11618-019-00904-1.
  - German overview of dropout: its extent, causes, consequences and prevention.
  - Access: verify. Springer page: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11618-019-00904-1
34. **Tight, M. (2020).** Student retention and engagement in higher education. *Journal of Further and Higher Education*, 44(5), 689–704. DOI 10.1080/0309877X.2019.1576860.
  - Systematic review: retention and engagement are two lenses on the same problem, and engagement has overtaken retention as a research focus.
  - Access: **OA (repository)** at https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/131095/1/tight.pdf
35. **Behr, A., Giese, M., Teguim Kamdjou, H. D., & Theune, K. (2020).** Dropping out of university: A literature review. *Review of Education*, 8(2), 614–652. DOI 10.1002/rev3.3202.
  - Groups determinants at three levels: the national system, the institution, and the student (before and during study). Dropout is a long decision process.
  - Access: verify (ERIC EJ1255295).
36. **Alyahyan, E., & Düştegör, D. (2020).** Predicting academic success in higher education: Literature review and best practices. *International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education*, 17, 3. DOI 10.1186/s41239-020-0177-7.
  - Step-by-step guidance for building student-success prediction models.
  - Access: **OA** at https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-020-0177-7
37. **de Oliveira, C. F., Sobral, S. R., Ferreira, M. J., & Moreira, F. (2021).** How does learning analytics contribute to prevent students' dropout in higher education: A systematic literature review. *Big Data and Cognitive Computing*, 5(4), 64. DOI 10.3390/bdcc5040064.
  - Systematic review of Web of Science and Scopus literature on learning analytics for dropout prevention.
  - Access: **OA** at https://doaj.org/article/a535ad2795ed43598748c51d22a8f307
38. **Shafiq, D. A., Marjani, M., Habeeb, R. A. A., & Asirvatham, D. (2022).** Student retention using educational data mining and predictive analytics: A systematic literature review. *IEEE Access*, 10, 72480–72503. DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3188767.
  - Reviews the factors, algorithms and methods used in retention prediction.
  - Access: **OA** (gold OA journal; DOI link).
39. **Rahmani, A. M., Groot, W., & Rahmani, H. (2024).** Dropout in online higher education: A systematic literature review. *International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education*, 21, 19. DOI 10.1186/s41239-024-00450-9.
  - PRISMA review of 110 studies (2013–2023) covering demographic, course, technology, motivation and support risk factors.
  - Access: **OA** at https://educationaltechnologyjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41239-024-00450-9

## C. RCTs and quasi-experimental causal evidence

40. **Dynarski, S. M. (2003).** Does aid matter? Measuring the effect of student aid on college attendance and completion. *American Economic Review*, 93(1), 279–288. DOI 10.1257/000282803321455287.
  - Uses the end of the Social Security student benefit as a natural experiment: removing the aid sharply reduced attendance and completion.
  - Access: **OA** at https://users.nber.org/~dynarski/2003%20Aid%20Matter.pdf (NBER w7422).
41. **Angrist, J., Lang, D., & Oreopoulos, P. (2009).** Incentives and services for college achievement: Evidence from a randomized trial. *AEJ: Applied Economics*, 1(1), 136–163. DOI 10.1257/app.1.1.136.
  - Canadian RCT: combining services with merit incentives raised women's grades and academic standing into year 2; no effect for men.
  - Access: **OA** at https://oreopoulos.faculty.economics.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/Incentives-And-Services-For-College-Achievement.pdf
42. **Lindo, J. M., Sanders, N. J., & Oreopoulos, P. (2010).** Ability, gender, and performance standards: Evidence from academic probation. *AEJ: Applied Economics*, 2(2), 95–117. DOI 10.1257/app.2.2.95.
  - Regression discontinuity: being placed on probation pushes some students out, raises GPA among those who stay, and lowers graduation for some groups.
  - Access: **OA** at https://njsanders.human.cornell.edu/Probation_AEJ_App.pdf
43. **Walton, G. M., & Cohen, G. L. (2011).** A brief social-belonging intervention improves academic and health outcomes of minority students. *Science*, 331, 1447–1451. DOI 10.1126/science.1198364.
  - One-hour belonging exercise (N = 92): over 3 years, African-American students' GPA rose and the achievement gap halved.
  - Access: OA copy (3rd-party) at https://orsl.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Walton-Cohen_2011-belonging-intervention.pdf
44. **De Paola, M., Scoppa, V., & Nisticò, R. (2012).** Monetary incentives and student achievement in a depressed labor market: Results from a randomized experiment. *Journal of Human Capital*, 6(1), 56–85. DOI 10.1086/664795.
  - Italian RCT: rewards improved performance of high-ability students only, and the effects persisted after the rewards ended.
  - Access: **paywalled**. Working paper at https://ideas.repec.org:443/p/clb/wpaper/201006.html
45. **Bettinger, E. P., & Baker, R. B. (2014).** The effects of student coaching: An evaluation of a randomized experiment in student advising. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis*, 36(1), 3–19. DOI 10.3102/0162373713500523.
  - InsideTrack coaching RCT: retention and completion rose and the effects persisted after coaching ended.
  - Access: **OA** at https://edpolicyinca.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/bettinger-effectsstudentcoaching-2014.pdf (NBER w16881).
46. **Scrivener, S., Weiss, M. J., Ratledge, A., Rudd, T., Sommo, C., & Fresques, H. (2015).** *Doubling Graduation Rates: Three-Year Effects of CUNY's Accelerated Study in Associate Programs (ASAP) for Developmental Education Students.* MDRC.
  - Comprehensive support (advising, tuition waiver, MetroCard, full-time requirement) nearly doubled 3-year graduation.
  - Access: **OA** at https://www.mdrc.org/sites/default/files/doubling_graduation_rates_fr.pdf
47. **Castleman, B. L., & Page, L. C. (2015).** Summer nudging: Can personalized text messages and peer mentor outreach increase college going among low-income high school graduates? *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 115, 144–160. DOI 10.1016/j.jebo.2014.12.008.
  - Low-cost text messages and peer outreach reduced "summer melt" (admitted students who never enrol).
  - Access: **paywalled**.
48. **Arnold, I. J. M. (2015).** The effectiveness of academic dismissal policies in Dutch university education: An empirical investigation. *Studies in Higher Education*, 40(6), 1068–1084. DOI 10.1080/03075079.2013.858684.
  - Binding study advice (BSA) raises first-year dropout by 6–7% and survivors' 4-year completion by 5–9%. Dropout happens earlier rather than less often.
  - Access: repository record (verify full text) at https://repub.eur.nl/pub/76454
49. **Bailey, T. R., Jaggars, S. S., & Jenkins, D. (2015).** *Redesigning America's Community Colleges: A Clearer Path to Student Success.* Harvard University Press. DOI 10.4159/9780674425934.
  - Makes the case for "guided pathways" in place of cafeteria-style course choice.
  - Access: **paywalled**.
50. **Goldrick-Rab, S., Kelchen, R., Harris, D. N., & Benson, J. (2016).** Reducing income inequality in educational attainment: Experimental evidence on the impact of financial aid on college completion. *American Journal of Sociology*, 121(6), 1762–1817. DOI 10.1086/685442.
  - Wisconsin randomised need-based grant: raised the odds of a 4-year bachelor's degree among Pell recipients.
  - Access: **OA (author)** at https://saragoldrickrab.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Goldrick-Rab-et-al-2016-Reducing-Income-Inequality-in-Educational-Attainment.pdf
51. **Castleman, B. L., & Long, B. T. (2016).** Looking beyond enrollment: The causal effect of need-based grants on college access, persistence, and graduation. *Journal of Labor Economics*, 34(4), 1023–1073. DOI 10.1086/686643.
  - Florida grant, regression discontinuity: $1,000 of grant eligibility raised 6-year BA completion by 4.6 percentage points.
  - Access: **OA** at https://www.nber.org/papers/w19306
52. **Logue, A. W., Watanabe-Rose, M., & Douglas, D. (2016).** Should students assessed as needing remedial mathematics take college-level quantitative courses instead? A randomized controlled trial. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis*, 38(3), 578–598. DOI: verify.
  - CUNY RCT, N = 907: corequisite statistics raised pass rates by 16 percentage points and credit accumulation compared with remedial algebra.
  - Access: **paywalled**. WWC review at https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/SingleStudyReviews/wwc_logue_031318.pdf
53. **Yeager, D. S., Walton, G. M., Brady, S. T., et al. (2016).** Teaching a lay theory before college narrows achievement gaps at scale. *PNAS*, 113(24), E3341–E3348. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1524360113.
  - Pre-matriculation belonging and growth-mindset modules reached more than 90% of entrants, raised full-time enrolment and reduced disadvantaged students' overrepresentation at the bottom of the class.
  - Access: **OA** at https://web.stanford.edu/~paunesku/articles/yeager_2016-pnas.pdf
54. **Sneyers, E., & De Witte, K. (2017).** The effect of an academic dismissal policy on dropout, graduation rates and student satisfaction: Evidence from the Netherlands. *Studies in Higher Education*, 42(2), 354–389. DOI 10.1080/03075079.2015.1049143.
  - Difference-in-differences: BSA raises first-year dropout and graduation rates and lowers overall satisfaction.
  - Access: **paywalled**.
55. **Page, L. C., & Gehlbach, H. (2017).** How an artificially intelligent virtual assistant helps students navigate the road to college. *AERA Open*, 3(4). DOI 10.1177/2332858417749220.
  - Georgia State "Pounce" chatbot RCT: enrolment rose 3.3 percentage points, a 21% reduction in summer melt.
  - Access: **OA** at https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1194134.pdf
56. **Dawson, S., Jovanović, J., Gašević, D., & Pardo, A. (2017).** From prediction to impact: Evaluation of a learning analytics retention program. *LAK '17*, 474–478. DOI 10.1145/3027385.3027405.
  - N = 11,160: an apparent retention effect of a predictive-analytics intervention disappears under more rigorous analysis. A cautionary result.
  - Access: repository record (verify full text) at https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/from-prediction-to-impact-evaluation-of-a-learning-analytics-rete/
57. **Broda, M., Yun, J., Schneider, B., Yeager, D. S., Walton, G. M., & Diemer, M. (2018).** Reducing inequality in academic success for incoming college students: A randomized trial of growth mindset and belonging interventions. *Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness*, 11(3), 317–338. DOI 10.1080/19345747.2018.1429037.
  - N = 7,686: growth mindset raised Latino/a students' GPA by about 0.40; the belonging arm had limited effects.
  - Access: OA copy (3rd-party) at https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/59faaf5b01b9500001e95457/5bc3d8c09f9d5c0fb16f0894_Broda,%20M.,%20Yun,%20J.,%20Schneider,%20B.,%20Yeager,%20D.%20S.,%20Walton,%20G.%20M.,%20&%20Diemer,%20M.%202018.pdf
58. **Clotfelter, C. T., Hemelt, S. W., & Ladd, H. F. (2018).** Multifaceted aid for low-income students and college outcomes: Evidence from North Carolina. *Economic Inquiry*, 56(1), 278–303.
  - UNC Carolina Covenant: grants alone had no effect; grants plus non-financial supports improved credit accumulation and GPA.
  - Access: **OA** at https://www.nber.org/papers/w22217
59. **Denning, J. T., Marx, B. M., & Turner, L. J. (2019).** ProPelled: The effects of grants on graduation, earnings, and welfare. *AEJ: Applied Economics*, 11(3), 193–224. DOI 10.1257/app.20180100.
  - Texas Pell discontinuity: extra grant aid raises completion and earnings and pays for itself within 10 years.
  - Access: **OA** at https://www.nber.org/papers/w23860
60. **Weiss, M. J., Ratledge, A., Sommo, C., & Gupta, H. (2019).** Supporting community college students from start to degree completion: Long-term evidence from a randomized trial of CUNY's ASAP. *AEJ: Applied Economics*, 11(3), 253–297. DOI 10.1257/app.20170430.
  - Graduation up 18 percentage points at 3 years and 10 percentage points at 6 years.
  - Access: **OA** at http://ungated-research.s3.amazonaws.com/pdf/2879-AEJ%20Applied%20Economics-2019-3-Supporting_Community_College_Students_from_Start_to_Degree_Completion__Long-Term_Evidence_from_a_Randomized_Trial_of_CUNY's_ASAP.pdf (ERIC ED604399).
61. **Oreopoulos, P., & Petronijevic, U. (2019).** *The Remarkable Unresponsiveness of College Students to Nudging and What We Can Learn from It.* NBER WP 26059.
  - About 25,000 students on 3 campuses: online and text nudges and coaching did not change academic outcomes, even for students at risk.

## D. Learning analytics, prediction and algorithmic fairness

78. **Yu, R., Lee, H., & Kizilcec, R. F. (2021).** Should college dropout prediction models include protected attributes? *L@S '21*. DOI 10.1145/3430895.3460139.

  - Access: **OA** at https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15237
79. **Bird, K. A., Castleman, B. L., Mabel, Z., & Song, Y. (2021).** Bringing transparency to predictive analytics: A systematic comparison of predictive modeling methods in higher education. *AERA Open*, 7. DOI 10.1177/23328584211037630.

  - Access: verify (ResearchGate copy).
83. **Bird, K. A., Castleman, B. L., & Song, Y. (2024).** Are algorithms biased in education? Exploring racial bias in predicting community college student success. *JPAM*, 44, 379–402. DOI 10.1002/pam.22569.
